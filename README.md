# tiny.detour — local traffic sandbox

With Node.js 18.17 or newer, run `npm start` in this folder and open http://127.0.0.1:4173/. No install or online service is required. Set `PORT` to use a different local port. Run `npm test` for the local regression checks, or `npm run benchmark` for a seeded traffic benchmark.

Choose a demo from the map selector. Elevation Crossing Lab shows traffic passing beneath a rising road at a fractional height. Each map's edits are saved locally in the browser; **Reset map** restores that demo's authored layout. The blank map and starter map are separate from the reference-inspired demos. **Blank map** asks before replacing an existing saved blank canvas.

## Building and editing

- New buildings must clear existing buildings and ground roads; new ground roads must clear buildings and cannot duplicate an existing road. Existing saved and demo layouts are not automatically changed.
- Choose **Draw road**, then Line, Curve, Ramp, or Circle. Ramp creates a one-way single-lane curve. A line takes two clicks; a curve or ramp takes a start, control point, and end; a circle takes a center and radius. When a ramp starts or ends on a road, click a lane to connect it; the highlighted lane and snap label show the selection.
- Select the end elevation before the final click. Start from a road at another elevation to build a straight or curved ramp. Elevated crossings and ground crossings remain separate. Water requires elevation, and mountains and ground-level forests block construction. Road previews, placement, and lane widening check the visible terrain outline across the road width.
- The floating road workbench has separate toggles for snapping to existing roads and for grid and alignment guides. When road snapping is enabled, junctions and road endpoints take priority. With guides enabled, snapped curves follow the connected road tangent, and ramps follow the selected lane’s direction. Grid, node-alignment, parallel, perpendicular, tangent, and 45-degree guides can be turned off in the workbench. Escape cancels an unfinished road.
- **Roadside access** controls where new buildings may be placed near roads. Choose both, left, right, or neither, then click a road or drag across several roads and release over a road; release elsewhere or press Escape to cancel. Green and red preview lines show allowed and blocked sides. Left and right follow the road’s original drawing direction. Press I to invert the selection. The protection checkbox blocks changes that would disable a side used by an existing building; when protection is off, those buildings keep their existing traffic access. Optional tool sounds are off by default. New roads have a separate building-side setting in the road workbench. Changes save locally and can be undone. Buildings outside the 100-unit road access range can still be placed disconnected.
- **Add lane** adds an outer lane on the chosen side; **Remove lane** removes the clicked lane. Click the start and end of the desired section, or drag between them. The first point chooses the side (and the lane when removing); Escape cancels the selection. Move an endpoint close to a junction until the Junction marker appears to make the lane reach it. Existing turn connections are shifted with the surviving lanes. **Replace lanes** applies the forward and reverse counts selected under Road design. The preview and edit follow the selected range up to the next junction, including across curved pieces. Lane layouts can have zero to three lanes in each direction, with at least one lane total.
- Select an intersection with **Inspect**. Click or drag from a colored incoming lane marker to an outgoing marker to toggle that connection. A removed connection can be added by repeating the action. Reset lane links returns that intersection to its default routing. Priority, yield and stop signs and timed lights are available. Lamps show green, amber and red. Signal intersections have an optional **Allow right on red** checkbox, off by default. Lane handles fan out with at least 16 screen pixels between them; thin guide lines point back to their physical lanes. Unequal opposing road widths blend into the junction with a smooth curb flare, including width changes just before the junction. By default, a one-lane slip road uses the outside lane of a wider road, straight movements preserve lane order, left turns use the inside lane and right turns use the outside lane. Explicit lane links override those defaults.
- Click a moving or waiting car with **Inspect** to see its remaining lane route, direction arrows and destination marker. The live card shows its destination, planned action, parking reservation and reason for waiting. The route updates after replanning.
- Choose **Move nodes** and drag a highlighted road point or junction. Connected road segments move together, and intersection rules and lane links are retained when they can be mapped safely. Boundary ports stay fixed. Intermediate curve handles are thinned at low zoom so they remain readable; zoom in to expose more points. Press Ctrl+Z to undo.
- **Remove** previews the affected portion of a road. Click or drag to bulldoze road sections and buildings. Use the undo button beside the playback controls or press Ctrl+Z to undo the latest map edit. Right-drag to pan with any tool; left-drag pans with tools that do not use dragging to edit. Scroll to zoom.

## Traffic behavior

- Unsigned intersections give way to the right. Left turns yield to oncoming straight traffic, including on green. Circulating roundabout traffic and through traffic at a main-road merge have priority. Signs can override an approach. After two seconds, stationary circular waits at equal priority resolve by arrival order; following vehicles cannot claim priority ahead of their lane's first vehicle.
- A signal's interval is its green time, followed by two seconds of amber and one second of all red before the perpendicular approaches turn green. Right on red requires a full one-second stop, an outside-lane right turn, a clear conflict path and available exit space. It is unavailable during amber or the all-red clearance interval.
- Green lights allow a moving stream to continue. Exit checks account for traffic clearing the junction rather than treating every downstream vehicle as a stopped queue. A stationary blocked exit still holds traffic. Vehicles retain conflicting-movement reservations until their rear clears the crossing.
- Drivers slow ahead of sharp turns and on roundabouts, maintain gaps based on vehicle length and relative speed, and plan lane changes across continuous runs of short road pieces. A lane change checks vehicles ahead and behind and reserves both lanes. Brake lights and turn indicators communicate the driver's actions.
- Vehicles reserve a real parking space before parking. If a destination is full, a departing car can free its original space and then look for an available alternative or a connected map exit. Searches are bounded and retried rather than running every frame. Departure checks include traffic on adjacent short road pieces. Existing waiting drivers receive newly freed spaces before fresh trips, and a full destination does not stop unrelated traffic from spawning.
- Editing intersection rules revalidates pending trips without resetting parked-car residence or departure animations. Arrival and departure use separate road anchors. If a departure becomes unreachable, its animation pauses rather than replaying; a valid route resumes it.

## Traffic implementation and measured performance

The audit found repeated global vehicle scans, repeated junction geometry and movement sampling, changing occupancy during a step, single-edge movement at short segment boundaries, overcautious exit blocking, and parking-animation resets on rule edits. These combined into unnecessary stops, inconsistent gaps, stalls, and costly updates on detailed interchanges.

The simulation now uses fixed steps of at most 50 ms, an immutable occupancy snapshot, numeric lane indexes, first-in-lane junction indexes, cached path distances and upcoming junctions/bends, cached junction dimensions, and cached movement geometry/conflict pairs. Movement consumes multiple short edges in a step. Replanning stays bounded, and traffic already committed to a crossing completes that movement before adopting new lane rules. Route display, lane-handle layout and driving rules have separate modules and focused regression scenarios.

`npm run benchmark` runs six demos for 300 simulated seconds each at 20 Hz using random seed 77. This same-machine example excludes graph construction and rendering. Behavior changes also affect vehicle counts and completed trips, so these are whole-simulation measurements, not a rendering FPS claim or a guarantee of equal work per tick.

| Map | Before mean tick, ms | After mean tick, ms | Trips before → after | Average vehicles before → after |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Starter | 0.302 | 0.200 | 277 → 245 | 26 → 27 |
| Coastal interchange | 0.146 | 0.123 | 99 → 88 | 5 → 11 |
| Cloverleaf | 1.862 | 0.619 | 269 → 383 | 102 → 103 |
| Five roundabouts | 0.497 | 0.577 | 247 → 241 | 24 → 33 |
| Garden district | 0.371 | 0.315 | 251 → 219 | 22 → 31 |
| Lane & ramp lab | 0.018 | 0.038 | 93 → 129 | 3 → 9 |

In this run, aggregate simulation time fell about 41%. Cloverleaf's mean step cost fell about 67% with similar average vehicle counts, while completed trips rose about 42%. Traffic generation now continues when an unrelated destination is full, so several demos run more vehicles; higher occupancy also means that some maps have more waiting or higher step costs. The run used the existing jam-removal safeguard ten times across the six maps; those removals are counted separately from successful trips.

`npm test` covers 73 scenarios, including green-light rolling flow versus stationary exit queues, right-on-red restrictions, right-of-way and circular waits, segment-independent motion, following across edge boundaries, parking capacity and animation state, existing drivers receiving freed spaces before new trips, continuous route previews, custom lane links, junction width flares and marker spacing.

## Current limitations

This is an early reference-inspired sandbox. The reference maps and junction shapes are approximations. Roads are stored as short linear segments, and lane-width transitions and highway ramps do not yet reproduce Trafficity's individual lane geometry. Lane edits do not yet draw independent tapered lane geometry for custom on- and off-ramps. Vehicles follow routes with lane-specific intersection connections, two-phase signals with amber/clearance intervals, and simplified merge priority. Signal phases group approaches by their final segment direction, and there are no protected turn phases or adaptive timings. A configured signal offset is respected, but there is no offset editor. Routes can plan gradual lane changes along continuous roads, including curves made from short segments. Vehicles check nearby traffic and reserve both lanes during a change; they do not change lanes through intersections. Congestion and conflict handling remain simplified. Road edits attempt to retain and reroute existing vehicles; vehicles that no longer fit the road network can be removed. Cars with no route retry for one minute before being removed; vehicles stalled for two minutes without meaningful movement are removed to clear a persistent jam. Traffic can still become congested. Elevation uses discrete visual layers with local depth handling at crossings and interpolated ramps rather than physical bridge structures. Terrain is seeded around fixed regions rather than fully procedural. Local saves are tied to their browser origin.

Keyboard shortcuts: 1–6 choose the first six tools, Ctrl+Z undoes an edit, Escape cancels the current operation, and I in Roadside access inverts the selected side. The proposed intersection marking editor is described in [INTERSECTION_MARKING_PLAN.md](INTERSECTION_MARKING_PLAN.md).
